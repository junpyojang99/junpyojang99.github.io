Main project, 2025–present

Moiré-modulated magnetic textures in Fe3GaTe2

Slight strain and twist between the layers of an Fe3GaTe2 flake can create a moiré pattern within the flake itself. In this van der Waals ferromagnet, which stays magnetic above room temperature, I study how the local moiré geometry relates to stripe domains, skyrmion lattices and their response to field.

I work across the project: preparing samples on TEM grids, measuring the magneto-optical Kerr effect (MOKE), planning and taking part in TEM and Lorentz TEM measurements, and analyzing both the moiré structure and the magnetic domains.

Poster at the MRS Fall Meeting 2026. One manuscript in preparation.

Software in Python and JavaScript

Analysis tools for TEM and Lorentz TEM

I write the analysis software for this project, and I check the moiré analysis on synthetic images where the answer is known.

  • Lorentz TEM tool (built on PyLorentz): phase and induction maps from the transport-of-intensity equation (TIE), stripe domain period and orientation, and skyrmion lattice statistics such as density, spacing, hexagonal order and size.
  • Moiré tool: maps of the local moiré period, direction, twist and strain across a flake.
  • Moiré simulator: synthetic TEM images of twisted and strained bilayers with known ground truth, used to validate the moiré analysis.

References. TIE: Paganin & Nugent, Phys. Rev. Lett. 80, 2586 (1998); Chess et al., Ultramicroscopy 177, 78 (2017). PyLorentz: McCray et al., Phys. Rev. Appl. 15, 044025 (2021). MuMax3: Vansteenkiste et al., AIP Adv. 4, 107133 (2014). Lock-in mapping: Hÿtch et al., Ultramicroscopy 74, 131 (1998); Benschop et al., Phys. Rev. Res. 3, 013153 (2021).

Samsung Electronics, 2023–2025

BEOL process integration, 1.4 nm logic node

As a back-end-of-line (BEOL) process integration engineer for this node, I developed via and metal stack schemes that lowered interconnect resistance, and helped improve yield by tracking down process issues. I verified each change with inline metrology, electrical tests and cross-sectional TEM.

Samsung Electronics, 2023–2025

Backside power delivery

I set up the backside BEOL route that delivers power from the back of the wafer, leaving the front side for signal wiring. I defined the process flow and specs with the unit process teams, ran wafers through the route and verified it with electrical tests, and solved the alignment problem between the nano through-silicon vias (nano-TSVs) and the first metal layer (M1) so that every backside power via landed on M1.

Process details are confidential, so the schematics show only public, generic concepts, not a Samsung process. Further reading: Semiconductor Engineering: interconnects (BEOL), imec: backside power delivery, Intel: PowerVia backside power delivery.
